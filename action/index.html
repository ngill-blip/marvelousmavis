Action

Mavis had a tragic end to her beautiful life on 3rd August 2026, twenty days before her twenty-first birthday. She was a member of an online forum where people in crisis are met with encouragement to die and with information on how to do it.

Her family has preserved what happened there, and is filing a lawsuit to ban the forum in India. Her posts from June and July 2026 are no longer publicly visible on the forum. Screenshots taken at the time record them — the thoughts she was having, the death she was planning, and the people around her trying to keep her safe. Others were posting the same things alongside her. Some of them are still there.

She was not the first to seek help on suicide on the site, but she should be the last.

If you or someone you know is having thoughts of suicide, please reach out. When we intervene at the right time, we may save a precious life.

Efforts to pull down the site have been successful
in the past in other countries

  • Ofcom · United Kingdom · 13 May 2026The UK’s communications regulator fined the forum’s provider £950,000 after finding illegal suicide content consistently present on the service, including content that encouraged or assisted suicide. Ofcom has linked the forum to more than 130 deaths in the UK, and is preparing a court application to have access blocked there. Ofcom’s decision
  • Rome prosecutors · Italy · June 2021Prosecutors ordered the site blocked after investigating the deaths of two Italian teenagers, reporting that members of the community had supplied information relating to suicide.
  • Australia and GermanyBoth have previously restricted access to the forum.
  • Scientific Reports · 2024A peer-reviewed study analysed more than 600,000 posts across nearly 40,000 threads by more than 11,000 users, and found that patterns of interaction there could identify users at heightened risk of suicide. Read the study
  • JMIR Mental Health · 2024A second peer-reviewed study identified the forum as a significant source of information about a substance used in suicides, including how to obtain it, and found users across numerous countries, India among them. Read the study

What happened to Mavis there has been compiled into a report: her preserved posts, evidence that the forum is still in active use in India, the ways it is found and named on other platforms, and the research and regulatory findings above.

The report carries other people’s words, written at the worst moments of their lives, by people who are still alive and still in danger. The report is available on request to journalists, investigators, regulators, lawyers, clinicians and bereaved families. Write to Jigeesha, and say who you are and what you need it for.
